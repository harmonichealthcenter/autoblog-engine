# Qiari Practitioner Tier vs Subscriber Tier: Which One Actually Fits You

Maya has been running a somatic coaching practice out of a converted studio in Portland for six years. Three months ago she started casually mentioning Qiari to her clients between sessions, "try a sleep session tonight and text me how you felt." About half of them actually did it. She has no idea which frequencies they picked, no idea what worked, no way to reference their experience in her next call with them. She's essentially running an informal referral program with zero feedback loop. Last week she opened a support chat and asked whether there was a version of Qiari built for people like her. There is. It's called the Qiari practitioner tier, and figuring out whether you need it comes down to a question most comparison articles skip entirely.

The question isn't which tier has more features. The question is whether you're a user of frequency wellness or a recommender of it. Everything else follows from that.

## What You're Actually Choosing Between

Qiari runs on a subscription model with a few different plans. The consumer-facing tiers, Basic, Core, and Ultra, give individuals access to personalized frequency wellness sessions they can run from a phone or laptop, no hardware required. The practitioner tier sits above these and is built for a different job: giving wellness professionals a structured way to bring frequency sessions into their client work.

Both tiers are device-free. Both deliver the same underlying session experiences. Both are subscription-based. So if you're only skimming feature lists, they can look like the same product with a different price tag. They aren't. They're built around two very different intents, and picking the wrong one either wastes money or leaves you unable to do the thing you actually wanted to do in the first place. Let's walk through what each one actually is.

## The Subscriber Tier: Personal Frequency Wellness With No Strings Attached

### What Subscribers Get, and What They Don't

The subscriber plans give you access to the Qiari session library, personalization based on your stated intentions (sleep, focus, calm, energy, recovery, and more), and the ability to run sessions on your own schedule from wherever you are. You pick what you want to work on, the app builds session experiences around that, and you engage with them for ten to thirty minutes at a time. You get progress tracking for yourself, session history, and the ability to layer intentions over time as your goals shift.

What you don't get on the subscriber tiers: any way to invite, manage, or track other people's sessions. Any client-facing dashboard. Any way to see what someone you recommended Qiari to actually experienced. This isn't a limitation the way "the free tier doesn't have X" is a limitation. It's a deliberate scoping decision. Subscribers are individuals working on themselves. That's the whole design.

### Price Point and Commitment

The subscriber tiers are priced for individuals doing personal wellness, and the monthly cost sits in the same range as most premium wellness apps, meditation apps, or a low-tier gym membership. There's no long-term contract locking you in, and you can move up or down between the consumer plans as your usage changes. If you find yourself only running two sessions a month, you can drop back. If you're doing daily sessions and want the full library, you move up.

### Who the Subscriber Tier Is Built For

The subscriber tier serves anyone whose relationship to Qiari begins and ends with their own experience. Biohackers experimenting with frequency as one input alongside sleep tracking, nutrition, and training. People who've heard about the Qi Coil ecosystem but aren't ready to invest in hardware and want a lower-friction way to feel what frequency wellness even is. Wellness-curious professionals who want a ten-minute wind-down session before bed. Travelers who used to rely on device-based practices and needed something app-native. If your answer to "who is this for" is "me," you're a subscriber.

## The Practitioner Tier: When You're Recommending Frequency to Other People

### Client Management and Session Tracking

The practitioner tier is where the product shape changes. Instead of a single-user experience, you get tools to bring clients into your Qiari environment, assign or recommend specific session types based on what you're working on with them, and see engagement data on their side. If a client you're coaching for sleep starts running the recommended sessions three nights a week, you know. If they don't, you know that too, and you can have an actual conversation about it in your next call instead of guessing. It helps to understand [how the underlying session experience is built](https://www.qiari.ai/) before you start deploying it into client work, because the way sessions are structured shapes how you'll want to talk about them.

This isn't surveillance. It's the same reason a strength coach wants to see whether the client actually did their programmed workouts. Without the feedback loop, you're just throwing suggestions into a void and hoping something sticks.

### The Business-Side Features That Matter

Practitioners also get support around integrating Qiari into an existing practice. That includes materials to explain the app to clients, session recommendations organized by common client presentations, and a professional context for how to talk about frequency wellness responsibly (which matters, because saying the wrong thing to a client can get you into trouble regardless of what tier you're on). There's also a community layer for practitioners, so you're not the only person in your city trying to figure out how to weave this into an existing modality. For practitioners who like to stay current on how the platform is thinking about client integration, [the ongoing writing on the Qiari blog](https://www.qiari.ai/blog) is worth keeping in your regular reading rotation.

### Pricing and ROI for Practitioners

The practitioner tier costs meaningfully more than the top consumer plan. That's the point. It's priced as a business tool, not a personal wellness subscription, and the math changes accordingly. If you have even five to ten clients regularly engaging with the sessions you recommend, and even a portion of them cite Qiari as part of why they're staying in your practice or renewing packages, the tier pays for itself. If you have zero clients using it, you're paying business-tool prices for a personal wellness product, which is the wrong trade every time.

## Where the Tiers Actually Differ: Format, Access, and Intent

### Hardware and Remote Delivery (Both Are Device-Free)

One of the more common misreadings of Qiari is that the practitioner tier involves some kind of hardware or in-person component. It doesn't. Both tiers deliver sessions through the same device-free, app-based format. The Qi Coil hardware side of the ecosystem is separate, and a practitioner doesn't need to own or recommend the coil to use the practitioner tier of Qiari. If you're a coach or wellness pro who has been curious about frequency but reluctant to stock physical devices, this matters, because the app-based delivery means your clients can engage from wherever they are, on whatever schedule works.

### Personalization Depth

Both tiers offer personalization, but the practitioner tier personalizes at a second level: you can shape what sessions get surfaced to a client based on what you're working on together. On the subscriber side, personalization is driven by the individual's own inputs. On the practitioner side, you become part of that input loop, which changes the quality of the recommendations a client receives.

### Community and Support Structures

Subscribers get consumer support and access to the general Qiari knowledge base. Practitioners get a more direct support channel and access to the practitioner community, which tends to matter more than people expect. Being able to ask another somatic coach or acupuncturist how they're framing frequency sessions to skeptical clients is worth something, and you can't get that from a FAQ page.

## Subscriber Tier Strengths: Why Some People Should Stay Here

The honest case for the subscriber tier is that it's the right answer for most people. If your relationship to frequency wellness is exploratory, personal, or one part of a broader biohacking stack, the subscriber plans give you everything you need at a price that respects the fact that you're a person, not a business. You don't need client management tools. You don't need business support materials. You need sessions you can run, and a way to track your own experience over time. The subscriber tier does that cleanly. If you want to poke around and see [what the Qiari app looks like from the user side](https://www.qiari.ai/) before committing, that's the starting point. Paying for the practitioner tier when you have no clients would be like buying commercial-grade espresso equipment for your kitchen counter. It's not that it doesn't work. It's that you're paying for capacity you'll never use.

## Practitioner Tier Strengths: What Makes It Worth the Upgrade

For a working practitioner, the calculus flips. The moment you're recommending Qiari to more than a handful of clients, the absence of a feedback loop becomes the problem, not a nice-to-have. You start hearing "yeah I've been doing the sessions" without any real signal about which sessions, how often, or what shifted. You end up recommending frequency the way you'd recommend a book, hoping something happens. The practitioner tier turns the recommendation into something you can actually integrate, follow up on, and refine. That's not a feature upgrade. That's a category change in how you can use the tool.

## The Real Decision: Are You Using Qiari or Recommending It

### If You're a Solo Wellness User or Biohacker

Stay on the subscriber tier. Pick the consumer plan that matches how often you're actually running sessions, and adjust up or down from there. If you're still on the fence about whether this fits into your existing stack, [browsing the case studies and thinking pieces on the blog](https://www.qiari.ai/blog) will give you a better sense of what people are actually doing with it. If you eventually want to explore hardware, the Qi Coil ecosystem is there, but the app on its own is a legitimate long-term practice.

### If You're Integrating Frequency Into a Practice

If you're a coach, therapist, wellness consultant, bodyworker, or any professional who's already recommending Qiari to clients informally, the practitioner tier is almost certainly the right move, and the tipping point comes earlier than most people think. You don't need fifty clients on it. You need enough that the tracking, the recommendation logic, and the professional context start doing real work in your practice.

### If You're Scaling a Frequency-Based Business

At this stage the practitioner tier isn't a question, it's infrastructure. You need the client management, you need the community, and you need to be inside a platform that's built for what you're doing rather than working around a consumer app.

## Which Tier Aligns With Your Wellness RESONANCE

The tier question really is a mirror question. Ask yourself who this is for, and the answer picks the plan. If it's for you and only you, the subscriber tiers are where your RESONANCE with the platform begins, and there's no reason to overspend on tools you won't use. If you're already showing up in client conversations recommending frequency sessions, the practitioner tier is what turns those recommendations into a coherent part of your work. Either way, the fastest way to know is to start where you honestly are today and let your usage tell you when it's time to move.
